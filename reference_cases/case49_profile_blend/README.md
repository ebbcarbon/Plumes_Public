# case49 — the non-default profiles on a merging plume, and the blend is the parabola's (2026-08-26)

Run to measure the one thing PLAN 8.4's rework had to *assume*: how the exe walks its
`3/2 Power law Profile` and `Gaussian Profile` from their round peak-to-mean to their slab
peak-to-mean as neighbouring plumes merge. case48 enumerated the three options on a geometry that
never merges, so only the round anchors were known; `crossplume.peak_to_mean` applied the parabola's
measured linear law between each profile's own anchors and said so. **That assumption is now a
measurement, and it holds to 0.002.** Ledger row 279.

Generated by `studies/profile_blend_experiment.py` as `pending/profile_merged_three_halves` and
`pending/profile_merged_gaussian`, run by the operator the same afternoon; the two notes carrying
the predictions registered before the runs are [`EXPERIMENT_NOTE_three_halves.md`](EXPERIMENT_NOTE_three_halves.md)
and [`EXPERIMENT_NOTE_gaussian.md`](EXPERIMENT_NOTE_gaussian.md).

## The runs

**case44's 0.60 m arm** (`spacing_0p6m.prj`, test79) regenerated from the case: 25 × 0.0127 m
ports, 0.60 m spacing, 45° up, 65° bearing into a 90° current, 0.005 m³/s at 35 psu, port at 2 m,
interval 1, 3 rise/falls, far-field stops 500 m / 10 000×. It merges at step 215 (`Dilutn` 69.5,
`d/L` 0.997) and reaches `d/L` 4.95, so both anchors and the whole walk sit in one trace.
[test79](../case44_spacing_sweep/test79.dat) is the **default-profile control** and was not rerun.

Three things worth keeping:

- **The blend is one law, not three.** The exe interpolates linearly in `d/L` between whatever the
  selected profile's two anchors are, exactly as it does for the parabola — the same shortcut past
  the geometric integral (row 204), now seen under three profiles. `peak_to_mean`'s assumption was
  right and its docstring's warning is retired.
- **The slab residuals are real and tiny.** 2.22200 against 20/9 = 2.22222 is −0.010 %, and
  2.14660 against 2.14715 is −0.026 % — flat to five figures over 132 rows each, so not noise, and
  the same order as the 3/2 option's +0.028 % round residual (row 278). Most likely the exe's own
  quadrature over the profile, again. The round plateaus reproduce case48's on a second geometry to
  the fourth decimal.
- **No overshoot here.** The banner fired at `d/L` 0.997 on this 65° diffuser, so the law starts
  within 0.005 of the round value and there is no ramp to speak of (compare the 45°/135° cases in
  PLAN 6b, where it starts at 2.08). The behaviour at the banner is the parabola's: round value on
  the banner row, the line from the row after.

## What the exe changed in the projects

Both `.prj` files came back rewritten (there is no Save-As; the exe writes the project on run):

- `nearfield_flags[1]` **1 → 0** in both. The note gave no surface-box instruction; ✅ **the
  operator unticked it by habit** (confirmed 2026-08-26). That completes row 187's reading:
  every `flags[1]` flip in the archive was a hand on the box — our README's instruction until
  2026-08-24, habit since — and never the exe. Nothing here depends on it (this plume never
  surfaces), and the generated run sheet now says *leave the box as loaded* so the habit cannot
  recreate the confound on a run where it would matter.
- the four near-field **plot** flags moved (`1,1,0,0` → `1,0,0,1`) — a GUI display choice, not a
  model input.
- the seven far-field flags are **unchanged** at `1,0,0,1,1,1,0`, the value every archived project
  with a far field carries. ⚠️ The GUI offers three eddy-diffusivity laws (`Constant`,
  `Linearly varying`, `4/3 power law` — the default, operator 2026-08-26) and every archived run
  used 4/3, so which flag position encodes the law was undecoded at the time. ✅ **Decoded by
  case50 (2026-08-26)**: far-field flags 2, 3 and 4 are the one-hot selector (Constant / Linearly
  varying / 4/3).

⚠️ The `.prj` carries **no record of the profile selected** (case48), so the option is recorded
here and in the filenames, by the person who made it.
