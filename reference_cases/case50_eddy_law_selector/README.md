output interval 1 — under the exe's two **non-default** far-field laws. Every archived far field
before this one had used the `4/3 power law` default, so which `.prj` field stores the selection,

## ⭐⭐ The selector: far-field flags 2, 3 and 4, one-hot

| run | seven far-field flags in the as-run `.prj` |
|---|---|
| every archived project (4/3 power law) | `1,0,0,1,1,1,0` |
| **Constant** | `1,1,0,0,1,1,0` |
| **Linearly varying** | `1,0,1,0,1,1,0` |

The note's prediction was "one position changes; position 4 is the standing guess". Two positions
change, because the encoding is one-hot — position 4 *is* the 4/3 flag, and positions 2 and 3 are
Constant and Linear. `io/prj.py`'s `farfield_flags` docstring now says so. Positions 1, 5, 6, 7 are
still unidentified.

## ⚠️⚠️ Row 280b: the manual's eq 11 has a stray factor of two, and the port had transcribed it

The linear-law **width** did not match until the port was changed. `farfield.brooks.width`
implemented the manual's `w/w₀ = 1 + 2βx/w₀` (MANUAL_DIGEST, eq 11) and read 176.1 / 307.5 /
701.5 m at the three stations — **57–87 % high**. The exe prints `w/w₀ = 1 + βx/w₀`, reproduced
to 1.7e-4 on every row.

The derivation sides with the exe: with `ε = ε₀ w/w₀` and `w = √12 σ`, `d(w²)/dt = 24 ε`
integrates to `w = w₀ + 12 ε₀ x/(u w₀) = w₀(1 + βx/w₀)`, and the same construction returns the
manual's constant and 4/3 forms exactly. Eq 15's erf argument, `(1 + βx/w₀)² − 1`, already carried
the right factor — which is why the linear *dilution* matched to 2e-4 before the width did. So the
typo is confined to eq 11, the port copied it faithfully, and no archived trace could have caught
it before today. Fixed in `farfield/brooks.py`; the manual's form is asserted to stay > 50 % high
inside row 280b's measurement so it cannot be quietly restored. SSMC report, Tier 3 item 19.

## Two reader defects these traces exposed, both fixed

1. `io/dat.py` required the word `based` in the far-field preamble (`4/3 Power Law based Eddy
   Diffusivity is used:`), which the other two forms lack. Both traces parsed with
   `farfield=None` and **no error**. The regex now takes `based` as optional and the writer
   (`format_eddy_law`) reproduces each of the three preambles byte for byte, refusing an unknown
   law rather than inventing a line.
2. The `.dat` reader's law string is now one of `4/3 Power Law` / `Constant` / `Linearly
   Varying`, the exe's own spellings.

## What it settles for the study

`EddyDiffusivityLaw` is a *site* decision (PLAN 8.5: 4/3 for open water, constant for
channelised receiving water). Until today only the default had a parity target; now all three
laws are confirmed against the exe on dilution and width, so choosing the constant law for a
channelised site costs no parity.
